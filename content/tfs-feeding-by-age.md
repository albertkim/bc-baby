# Feeding by Age and Meal and Snack Ideas

_Source pages: 54-58_

_Handbook: Toddler’s First Steps, 4th edition, updated in 2024._

## Feeding by Age

### 6 to 9 Months

Your baby may:

- Continue to breastfeed or chestfeed.
- Pick up food between their fingers and palms and put it in their mouth.
- Bite off food.
- Close their lips around a cup held for them.
- Chew by munching up and down, and by moving food from front to back and to the sides of their mouth.

Human milk is still your child’s most important drink.

- At about 6 months, begin introducing finger foods and foods that are soft, lumpy, mashed, ground, pureed, finely minced and tender-cooked.
- Start with iron-rich foods, offering them 2 or more times a day, then add variety.
- Offer solid foods before or after breastfeeding or chestfeeding, depending on what works best. This may change over time.
- Offer family foods made with little or no added salt or sugar. Start with small amounts and offer more based on hunger and fullness cues.
- Give sips of clean water from an open cup, but do not let them fill up on water. Give lots of practice feeding themself.
- Sit down and eat together as often as possible.
- By 9 months, offer 2 to 3 meals and 1 to 2 snacks a day, in addition to human milk.
- Introduce common allergens one at a time: milk and milk products, eggs, thinly spread peanut butter and tree-nut butters, soy, seafood (fish and shellfish), wheat and sesame (see Understanding Food Allergies).
- Be aware of choking hazards (see Reducing Choking Hazards).
- Do not give honey or food made with honey, even if cooked or pasteurized (see Safe, Healthy Foods and Drinks). Avoid sugary drinks, including 100% fruit juice.

Instead of jarred baby food, try mashing soft, cooked family foods with a fork. Add water or human milk to moisten, and try mixing different foods together.

### 9 to 12 Months

Your baby may:

- Bite and chew up and down.
- Use their thumb and fingers to pick up small pieces of food.
- Hold a cup in 2 hands.
- Try to use a spoon.
- Drop things from their feeding chair.
- Want to sit at the family table and try to feed themself.
- Offer 3 meals and 1 or 2 sit-down snacks at regular times each day. Sit and eat with your toddler.
- Offer solid foods before or after breastfeeding or chestfeeding, whichever works better; this may change over time.
- Offer iron-rich foods 2 or more times a day. Keep offering new family foods with different textures, such as chopped, mashed or shredded. Offer family foods without added sugar or salt.
- Give small amounts of clean water in an open cup; encourage self-feeding and let your toddler decide how much to eat.
- Be aware of choking hazards (see Reducing Choking Hazards). Do not give honey or food made with honey, even if cooked or pasteurized (see Safe, Healthy Foods and Drinks).

### 12 to 24 Months

Your toddler may:

- Feed themself—messily—with their fingers or a spoon.
- Bite and chew food more easily.
- Eat a variety of foods with different textures.
- Eat very little or a lot, depending on the day.
- Play with their food.
- Need to be offered a new food many times before trying it.
- Eat most foods without coughing or gagging.

**Family foods** are foods the whole family normally eats at mealtime. Giving a toddler foods from the family table instead of store-bought baby food provides a wider range of textures, tastes and nutrients.

- Offer 3 meals and 2 or 3 sit-down snacks at regular times each day, and include your toddler in regular family meals.
- Include iron-rich foods 2 or more times a day.
- Offer a variety of textures, including finger foods, and family foods with little or no added salt or sugar.
- Offer new foods alongside other foods they like. Continue offering rejected foods without pressure; give the same foods prepared in different ways.
- Include healthy higher-fat foods such as salmon, avocado, cheese and nut butters.
- Give water between meals and snacks, enough time to eat, and let your toddler decide how much to eat.
- Continue breastfeeding or chestfeeding and be aware of choking hazards (see Reducing Choking Hazards).
- Limit foods high in salt and sugar, such as chips, candy and cookies. Limit sugary drinks such as fruit juice, pop, sports drinks, energy drinks and fruit-flavoured beverages. Avoid all foods and drinks with caffeine or artificial sweeteners.

Include your toddler at the family table and eat together as often as possible. Shared meals help them learn language and social skills and get used to family foods. Talk about what they see, taste and feel.

Help your toddler get enough iron by offering iron-rich foods 2 or more times a day at meals or snacks: meat, poultry, fish, iron-fortified infant cereals, cooked eggs and tofu, and mashed, cooked beans and other legumes.

### 24 to 36 Months

Your toddler may:

- Hold a cup, but spill a lot.
- Feed themself cut-up family foods.
- Eat very little or a lot, depending on the day.
- Show strong food likes and dislikes.
- Need to be offered a new food many times before trying it.
- Offer 3 meals and 2 or 3 sit-down snacks at regular times each day. Include your toddler in regular family meals and eat together as often as possible.
- Offer a variety of foods from Canada’s food guide.
- Plain lower-fat cow’s milk (1% or 2%) or plain fortified soy beverage can begin to replace whole (3.25%) cow’s milk.
- Include high-energy, healthy-fat foods such as avocado, fatty fish, thinly spread nut and seed butters, and vegetable oils.
- Continue breastfeeding or chestfeeding for as long as you and your child want. Offer water between meals and snacks. Let your toddler decide how much to eat.
- Be aware of choking hazards (see Reducing Choking Hazards).
- Limit foods high in salt and sugar, such as chips, candy and cookies. Limit sugary drinks such as fruit juice, pop, sports drinks, energy drinks and fruit-flavoured beverages. Avoid all foods and drinks with caffeine or artificial sweeteners.

Fruit juice is a sugary drink, and children do not need it. If offered, limit it to no more than 125 ml (½ cup) a day and serve it with a meal or snack in an open cup.

**Family story:** “I was amazed at how much better my son ate when we sat together and had the same foods. I made meals we could both eat and cut his up into small pieces he could pick up. It was so cute watching him study—and copy—how I ate.”

**Try this:** Start each feeding with a small amount of food. Give more based on hunger and fullness cues (see Eating Well). Appetite normally changes from day to day; trust that your toddler will eat the amount they need.

### Sample One-Day Menu for a 24- to 36-Month-Old

- **Breakfast:** Oatmeal with sliced blueberries or banana; plain cow’s milk (1%, 2% or 3.25%) or plain fortified soy beverage.
- **Snack 1:** Banana bread thinly spread with peanut or nut butter; water.
- **Lunch:** Salmon or egg salad sandwich on whole-wheat bread; finely chopped or very thinly sliced red peppers and cucumbers; plain cow’s milk (1%, 2% or 3.25%) or plain fortified soy beverage; canned peaches.
- **Snack 2:** Grated cheese; finely chopped or very thinly sliced apples; water.
- **Dinner:** Whole-wheat spaghetti with tomato and meat or lentil sauce; bite-sized pieces of cooked carrot and broccoli florets; plain cow’s milk (1%, 2% or 3.25%) or plain fortified soy beverage.

## Daily Food Suggestions

Offer your toddler a variety of healthy family foods plus human milk every day. The following source table is based on Canada’s food-guide groupings.

### 6 to 9 Months

- **Meals and snacks:** 2 to 3 solid-food feedings and 1 to 2 snacks. Continue breastfeeding or chestfeeding. If the child is not breastfeeding or chestfeeding, give store-bought infant formula.
- **Vegetables and fruit:** Soft-cooked vegetables; soft fruits such as banana and kiwi; canned fruit; peeled, pitted and cooked hard fruits such as apple and pear.
- **Whole grains:** Iron-fortified infant cereal; cooked rice and pasta; oat-ring cereal; crackers, roti and pita.
- **Protein foods:** Tender-cooked, finely minced or shredded meat, poultry, fish or shellfish; mashed or diced cooked egg, tofu or legumes; peanut or nut butter spread thinly on toast or crackers; yogurt, cottage cheese and grated cheese.

### 9 to 12 Months

- **Meals and snacks:** 3 solid-food feedings and 1 to 2 snacks. Continue breastfeeding or chestfeeding. If the child is not breastfeeding or chestfeeding, small amounts of whole (3.25%) cow’s milk can start (see Safe, Healthy Foods and Drinks).
- **Vegetables and fruit:** Soft-cooked vegetables; grated raw vegetables; cooked or grated hard fruits; soft fruits such as banana and kiwi; canned fruit; peeled, pitted and cooked hard fruits such as apple and pear.
- **Whole grains:** Iron-fortified infant cereal; whole-grain toast, pasta, rice and crackers; small pieces of bannock, tortillas, roti and other breads.
- **Protein foods:** Small, tender-cooked pieces of meat, poultry, fish or shellfish; mashed or diced cooked egg, tofu and legumes; peanut or nut butter spread thinly on toast or crackers; yogurt, cottage cheese, grated cheese and whole (3.25%) cow’s milk.

### 12 to 24 Months

- **Meals and snacks:** 3 meals and 2 to 3 snacks. Continue breastfeeding or chestfeeding. If not breastfeeding or chestfeeding, offer 2 cups (500 ml) of whole (3.25%) cow’s milk or soy-based infant formula with meals (see Safe, Healthy Foods and Drinks).
- **Vegetables and fruit:** Small pieces of soft fruits and vegetables; cooked or grated vegetables; cooked or grated hard fruits.
- **Whole grains:** Whole-grain hot and cold cereal; brown rice, whole-grain pasta and congee; whole-grain crackers and toast; whole-grain bannock, tortillas, roti and other breads.
- **Protein foods:** Tender-cooked ground, chopped or cubed lean meat, poultry, fish or shellfish; mashed legumes; whole cooked egg; cooked tofu; peanut or nut butter spread thinly on toast or crackers; yogurt, cottage cheese, grated cheese, whole (3.25%) cow’s milk or soy-based infant formula.

### 24 to 36 Months

- **Meals and snacks:** 3 meals and 2 to 3 snacks. Continue breastfeeding or chestfeeding if you wish. If not breastfeeding or chestfeeding, offer 2 cups (500 ml) of plain cow’s milk (1%, 2% or 3.25%) or plain fortified soy beverage each day with meals (see Safe, Healthy Foods and Drinks).
- **Vegetables and fruit:** Small pieces of soft fruits and vegetables; cooked or grated vegetables; cooked or grated hard fruits.
- **Whole grains:** Whole-grain hot and cold cereal; brown rice, whole-grain pasta and congee; whole-grain crackers and toast; whole-grain bannock, tortillas, roti and other breads.
- **Protein foods:** Tender-cooked ground, chopped or cubed lean meat, poultry, fish or shellfish; mashed legumes; whole cooked egg; cooked tofu; peanut or nut butter spread thinly on toast or crackers; yogurt, cottage cheese, grated cheese, plain cow’s milk (1%, 2% or 3.25%), or plain fortified soy beverage.

## Healthy Meal and Snack Ideas

**Canada’s food guide** provides information to help plan nutritious meals and snacks; see the appendix. Offer your toddler the same healthy foods the rest of the family enjoys. Eat vegetables, fruit, whole grains and protein foods regularly, and offer different flavours and textures.

- Baked beans on whole-grain toast.
- Carrot or pumpkin bread with cream cheese.
- Chili, dahl or lentils with rice and vegetables.
- Congee or rice porridge with small pieces of meat.
- Dessert tofu with fresh fruit.
- Fish in a whole-wheat bun or bannock.
- Soft fresh fruit pieces, with seeds and tough skins removed, and yogurt for dipping.
- Grated cheese with whole-grain crackers.
- Macaroni and cheese.
- Meatballs with pasta.
- Milk or yogurt blended with fruit.
- Oatmeal or cream of wheat with milk.
- Pancakes or waffles with applesauce.
- Rice or pasta with tender-cooked meat and small bits of vegetables.
- Rice pudding made with milk.
- Scrambled eggs or mini omelette made with diced vegetables.
- Small muffin thinly spread with peanut or nut butter.
- Soft tortillas filled with beans or ground meat.
- Sandwich triangles with egg, canned tuna or salmon, or easy-to-chew meat.
- Fish chowder and strips of toast.
- Spaghetti with tomato or meat sauce.
- Vegetable, split-pea or bean soup with whole-wheat crackers, bannock or roti.
- Cooked and cooled vegetables with yogurt dip, hummus or dahl.
- Whole-grain cold cereal with milk and fruit pieces or berries.
- Whole-grain crackers or rice cakes with cottage cheese or mashed avocado.
- Whole-wheat pita or roti with hummus or dahl.
- Yogurt with pieces of soft fruit or applesauce, or yogurt with crackers or roti.
